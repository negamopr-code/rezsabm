PART A — handbook chapter content

### Setup
*   **Instrument**: **Tesla (TSLA) stock** (also referred to as "test shares" in the transcript) [1, 2].
*   **Structure**: A **modified bear put spread** (collared structure) designed to hedge existing shares [3, 4]. The setup consists of owning stock, buying a protective put right below the stock open price, selling a put at a lower support strike (creating the bear put spread), and simultaneously selling an out-of-the-money call to finance the protection [2, 4].
*   **Strikes/Deltas**: 
    *   **Long Put**: Bought **5 of the 250 puts** [2].
    *   **Short Put**: Sold **5 of the 195 puts** (placed **55 points below** the 250 puts) [2].
    *   **Short Call**: Sold **5 of the 265 calls** [2].
*   **DTE (Days to Expiration)**: Expiring "**a week later on February 16th**" (quoted exactly as transcribed on a trade entered December 20th) [2].
*   **Entry Trigger**: A **parabolic stock run-up** where an investor has large, embedded, unrealized gains but wants to remain long to capture future upside, while seeking a downside hedge to mitigate a sharp pullback without triggering capital gains taxes [5, 6].

### Management and Exit Rules
*   **Consolation Zone**: If the stock settles **between 250 and 265** at expiration, all options expire worthless, allowing the trader to simply keep the original positive cash flow as a consolation prize while retaining the stock [6].
*   **Downside Protection**: If the stock pulls back, the 250 put begins to gain value, effectively offsetting the paper drawdown on the underlying stock [6, 7].
*   **Profit Cap on Downside**: Downside options profits stop increasing once the stock drops to or below the **195 strike price**, which activates the short put leg [6].
*   **Upside Cap**: If the stock rallies past the **265 call strike**, the shares are called away at that price [2, 4].

### Stated Edge or Statistics
*   **Hedge Sizing**: Position hedged is **500 shares** with an original cost basis of **about \$110** (representing an original investment of **about \$55,000**) [2, 7].
*   **Hedge Financing Cash Flow**: Selling the 5 calls "**up at 65**" (garbled/as spoken) yielded a "**credit of 16005**" (garbled/as spoken), creating an upfront positive cash flow of "**actually \$825**" to finance the put protection [4].
*   **Historical Moves**: In the first half of 2024, Tesla opened at **250**, plummeted to a low of **13880** in mid-April, and surged back to **260** by July 15th (representing a **90% rally off its lows**) [3]. Prior to the December 20, 2023 trade, it staged a **26% rally** off its October lows [2].

### Caveats
*   **Tax Drag of Outright Selling**: Exiting a bullish position outright to protect gains triggers premature capital gains taxes [5].
*   **Unhedged Vulnerability**: Tesla historically follows major parabolic runs with devastating pullbacks [1].
*   **Friction & Clerical Discrepancies**: Options pricing and timelines contain transcribed clerical errors (such as stating an options chain from December 20th to February 16th expired "**a week later**" [2], and listing call sales "**up at 65**" with a contract credit of "**16005**" generating only "**\$825**" in cash flow [4]).
