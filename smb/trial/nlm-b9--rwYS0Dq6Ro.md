### Management and Exit Rules
*   **Target Profit**: \$500 or 10% of capital.
*   **Stop Loss (Maximum Loss Limitation)**: \$500 per trade.
*   **Mid-Trade Drawdown Tolerance**: Expect to experience a normal mid-trade drawdown of \$400 during the course of the trade. The trade should be allowed to run and recover to hit its target rather than panicking out early.
*   **Capital Sizing Discipline (The Core Rule)**: Do not scale capital drastically following a short, predictable winning streak (such as six months without a loss). Sizing up must be treated like "weight training," scaling up "very very gradually over a number of years" by making minor incremental increases (e.g., starting with 50 pounds, bumping the weight 5 pounds at a time—a 10% increase—and gradually building risk tolerance like a muscle).

### Stated Edge or Statistics
*   **6-Month Campaign Performance**: On \$5,000 of starting capital, a systematic campaign yielded a profit of \$1,000 (a 20% return) over a six-month period.
*   **Win Rate**: The strategy won four out of six months (Month 1: up \$350, Month 2: made \$500, Month 3: lost \$500, Month 4: made \$350, Month 5: lost \$100, Month 6: made \$400).
*   **Drawdown Frequency**: In three out of the six months (Months 2, 4, and 6), the trade experienced an interim drawdown of \$400 before recovering to make a profit.

### Caveats
*   **The ATM Machine Illusion**: Options income trading is a statistical game with predictable, inevitable losses occurring every year. Cockiness after an initial winning streak (such as six months of consecutive wins) is highly dangerous; no options trading style acts as an automated "ATM machine."
*   **The Psychological Sizing Trap**: Increasing capital 100x overnight (from \$5,000 to \$500,000) alters the psychological and emotional nature of the trade. A completely normal \$400 mid-trade drawdown is scaled into a \$40,000 drawdown. Aspiring traders who cannot handle those dollar values will panic and close the trade early to relieve anxiety, turning normal drawdowns into massive realized losses.
*   **Catastrophic Over-Sizing Outcome**: In the scaled-up \$500,000 scenario, panicking and exiting at the \$40,000 drawdown level on every trade results in losing on five out of six trades, creating a disastrous 27% loss (-\$135,000) instead of the expected 20% positive return (+\$100,000).
*   **Psychological Scarring**: Scaling up too quickly creates severe psychological scars and deep-seated fear. The trader permanently "trades scared" (becoming too gun-shy to take necessary risks) or blows up their capital entirely, destroying their career.
