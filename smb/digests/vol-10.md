### NEW TOPICS NOT IN TAXONOMY

#### NEW:slide_errors (Estimated Count: 55 comments)
Audience members consistently spot visual, textual, and mathematical errors in the PowerPoint presentation slides used during tutorials [17, 34, 50]. Viewers complain that short calls are labeled as puts [34], wrong strike numbers are written in formulas (such as utilizing the 10-wide math on 20-wide spread slides) [50], and margin requirements are grossly miscalculated (such as stating the margin on an 8-contract spread was \$5,848 when the presenter later admits "Tim I booted it on this one and you are correct about the margin") [17, 32]. Beginner traders note that these typos severely disrupt the learning process because the on-screen numbers contradict the spoken audio explanation [50].

#### NEW:buy_and_hold_comparison (Estimated Count: 35 comments)
A recurring mathematical argument from the audience is that the active options strategies presented—such as covered calls, naked put selling, or index condor campaigns—regularly underperformed a simple "buy-and-hold" strategy of broad-market index ETFs (like SPY or QQQ) or strong individual equities (like TSLA) [38, 40, 44]. Commenters demonstrate that holding shares outright carries zero management stress, avoids exchange commissions, captures massive upside surges, and qualifies for long-term capital gains tax benefits rather than being heavily taxed under short-term options income rates [40, 47].

#### NEW:proprietary_trigger (Estimated Count: 35 comments)
Skeptical options traders point out a massive logical gap in the presentations: the high win rates and backtested profits shown on spreads are not a function of the options structure itself, but rather rely on a "proprietary trigger" or signal to determine entry direction [2, 3]. Viewers complain that they are shown "half the story" when presenters demonstrate a perfect win rate using an undisclosed direction-pointing indicator, noting that the firm is essentially utilizing these videos as soft-sales pitches to direct retail traders to pay up to \$2,500/year for their "IQ Signal" service [18, 51].

#### NEW:payoff_graphs (Estimated Count: 25 comments)
The public frequently requests that the presenters include visual risk payoff diagrams (P&L curves) mapping out breakeven zones, max loss clifftops, and max profit areas on options chains [8, 44, 52]. Visual learners argue that options matrices and text-heavy tables are very dry and difficult to visualize [53]. They advocate that showing how the risk curve shifts over time in software like OptionVue or Thinkorswim would instantly clarify the strategies to novices far better than mathematical tables [53].
